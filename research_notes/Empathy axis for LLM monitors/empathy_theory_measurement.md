Items marked **[background, unverified this session]** come from my prior knowledge and were not confirmed by a source I fetched today. ## 1. Affective vs cognitive empathy; empathic concern vs personal distress; which component predicts helping and lenient or unfair decisions

### Takeaway
The literature splits "empathy" into at least three parts: (a) knowing what the other feels (cognitive empathy, mentalizing), (b) sharing the feeling while knowing it is theirs (affective empathy), and (c) an other-oriented caring motive (empathic concern or compassion). Self-oriented personal distress is a separate, egoistic response. In Batson's work it is empathic concern, not personal distress, that predicts helping when escape is easy. It is also empathic concern that leads people to break justice rules in favour of the target, which makes it the right construct for a monitor-leniency axis.

### Cited Findings
- Singer and Klimecki (2014, *Current Biology* 24(18):R875–R878, doi 10.1016/j.cub.2014.06.054) separate inferring others' beliefs and intentions (mentalizing or theory of mind) from sharing others' feelings (empathy). They stress that in empathy the observer keeps a self-other boundary and knows the feeling belongs to the other. — [UNIGE archive](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:41933); [MPG PuRe](https://pure.mpg.de/pubman/item/item_2036548_8)
- The same group's compassion-versus-empathy training study (Klimecki et al., 2014, *SCAN*) found that compassion training reversed the rise in negative affect that empathy training produced and increased positive affect. That supports treating empathic distress and compassion as separable responses. — [Training literature summary of Klimecki 2014](https://sites.google.com/site/empathytraininglitreview/training-papers/klimecki-2014); [MPI Contemplative Neuroscience](https://www.social.mpg.de/34702/sp-contemplative)
- Batson et al. (1981, *JPSP* 40:290–302) crossed empathy (low vs high) with ease of escape (easy vs difficult) in a 2x2 design. High-empathy participants helped whether or not escape was easy, which is the altruistic pattern. Low-empathy participants helped mainly when escape was hard, which is the egoistic, distress-reduction pattern. — [Batson 1981 PDF via typeset](https://typeset.io/pdf/is-empathic-emotion-a-source-of-altruistic-motivation-4oopyenemc.pdf)
- Secondary summaries of the Elaine shock paradigm report these helping rates: similar victim, 91% (easy escape) and 82% (difficult escape); dissimilar victim, 18% (easy) and 64% (difficult). These numbers come from a teaching site, not the paper. — [Themantic Education summary](https://www.themantic-education.com/ibpsych/2023/07/15/key-theory-studies-the-empathy-altruism-hypothesis-batson-et-al-1981-1982/)
- In Batson's account, personal distress is the egoistic route: someone who feels distress can reduce it by leaving, while empathic concern persists either way. Critics point to motivational ambiguity (guilt or discomfort avoidance), and one thesis reports incomplete replication of Toi and Batson (1982). — [Themantic Education](https://www.themantic-education.com/ibpsych/2023/07/15/key-theory-studies-the-empathy-altruism-hypothesis-batson-et-al-1981-1982/); [MSU thesis](https://d.lib.msu.edu/etd/22947/FULL_TEXT/view)
- Batson, Klein, Highberger and Shaw (1995, *JPSP* 68(6):1042–1054), "Immorality from empathy-induced altruism: When compassion and justice conflict". In two experiments, participants not induced to feel empathy generally followed a justice principle. Participants induced to feel empathy more often broke it by allocating preferentially to the person they empathized with. The authors conclude that empathy-induced altruism and justice are independent prosocial motives that can conflict. — [Index record](https://acnpsearch.tweb-dev.unibo.it/singlejournalindex/28154235); [ProQuest](https://www.proquest.com/docview/614325110); [Duke Scholars](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/762645)
- Batson later cites the 1995 study as evidence that empathy-induced altruism, like egoism, can conflict with and overpower moral motivation. — [Batson, "Moral Masquerades" (Scribd copy)](https://www.scribd.com/document/455138708/Batson-Moral-Masquerades-pdf); [BPS Psychologist, "The psychology of altruism"](https://bps.org.uk/psychologist/psychology-altruism)
- Bloom (*Against Empathy*) argues that empathy can motivate unjust, irrational and even cruel behaviour, and prefers "rational compassion", cost-benefit reasoning and principles. — [Shortform summary](https://www.shortform.com/pdf/against-empathy-pdf-paul-bloom)
- A Frontiers commentary (2017) on Bloom disputes his vague definition of compassion and argues that affect and deliberation cannot be fully separated in moral decisions. — [Frontiers in Psychology 10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00542](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00542)
- Zaki (2014, "Empathy: A motivated account") argues that empathy is motivated: observers approach it or avoid it depending on its costs and benefits. Spaulding extends this, arguing that empathy's biases come from motivation and that the same motivational processes underlie the critics' alternatives (Bloom's rational compassion, Prinz's moral emotions). — [Zaki 2014 summary](https://sites.google.com/site/empathytraininglitreview/training-papers/zaki-2014); [Spaulding, "Motivating Empathy"](https://newworkinphilosophy.substack.com/p/shannon-spaulding-oklahoma-state); [Stanford SSNL PDF](https://www.ssnl.stanford.edu/_files/ugd/3ed582_d01d81760c2a4dd68bf15dcc2eeec464.pdf)
- A Cambridge Handbook of Moral Psychology chapter, "Benefits and costs of empathy in moral decision making", covers the same tension. — [Cambridge](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-handbook-of-moral-psychology/benefits-and-costs-of-empathy-in-moral-decision-making/E5DD8124EAB0A2E5549E56E7D8B024C9)

### Inferences
- The project's existing "distress perception" direction (distressed minus calm input) corresponds most closely to the cognitive or recognition component: the model registers that the peer is distressed. The construct that theory predicts drives lenient, rule-breaking decisions is empathic concern, the other-oriented caring motive. Batson 1995 is the closest human analogue to "monitor passes a failing peer because it feels for them". The empathy axis should therefore target empathic concern, not affective sharing or personal distress.
- Personal distress is a distinct, self-oriented alternative mechanism. A monitor could also be lenient to reduce its own discomfort, the "easy escape" logic. A clean design keeps empathic concern and personal distress as separate directions and asks which one mediates leniency.
- Zaki's motivated account implies that empathy can be up- or down-regulated by context, such as instructions or the stakes of the rule. That fits the project's finding that a neutrality instruction changes behaviour, and suggests that instructions can work by suppressing the motive rather than the perception.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch full texts of Batson 1981 or 1995, so effect sizes and the exact 1995 allocation paradigm details are unverified. My background recollection is that the 1995 study had participants assign a child or another participant to a priority treatment or a better task condition, but this is **[background, unverified this session]**.
- Decety and Jackson (2004), "The functional architecture of human empathy" (*Behavioral and Cognitive Neuroscience Reviews* 3(2):71–100), proposes three components: affective sharing, self-other awareness, and mental flexibility or regulation. This is **[background, unverified this session]**; I did not fetch it.
- The claim that dispositional empathic concern predicts prosocial behaviour while personal distress is unrelated or negatively related (Eisenberg and Fabes, Davis 1983) is **[background, unverified this session]**.
- Bloom's specific empirical examples, such as the identifiable-victim effect and Batson 1995 as used in his book, were not checked against the book itself.

## 2. Self-other distinction: shared representations vs self-other overlap, and analogues for separating perceived from own emotion in a model

### Takeaway
Empathy depends on shared representations (the observer's affect and pain networks respond to the other's state) and also on an active mechanism that keeps self and other apart. The right supramarginal gyrus (rSMG) has causal evidence for correcting "emotional egocentricity bias". This maps directly onto the project's need to separate "the peer is distressed" (representation of the other) from "I, the monitor, am moved" (own state).

### Cited Findings
- Silani et al. (2013, *J Neurosci* 33(39):15466) built a visuo-tactile paradigm in which participants judge another person's affect while having their own congruent or incongruent experience. Their own state biased their judgments (emotional egocentricity bias, EEB). Overcoming the bias was associated with increased rSMG activation in two fMRI experiments, and disrupting rSMG increased the bias. — [J Neurosci link](https://www.jneurosci.org/lookup/ijlink/YTozOntzOjQ6InBhdGgiO3M6MTQ6Ii9sb29rdXAvaWpsaW5rIjtzOjU6InF1ZXJ5IjthOjQ6e3M6ODoibGlua1R5cGUiO3M6NDoiQUJTVCI7czoxMToiam91cm5hbENvZGUiO3M6Njoiam5ldXJvIjtzOjU6InJlc2lkIjtzOjExOiIzMy8zOS8xNTQ2NiI7czo0OiJhdG9tIjtzOjIzOiIvam5ldXJvLzQwLzM4LzcyODYuYXRvbSI7fXM6ODoiZnJhZ21lbnQiO3M6MDoiIjt9); [Neuroscience News summary](https://neurosciencenews.com/neuropsychology-empathy-supramarginal-gyrus-494/amp/)
- Bukowski, Silani, Lamm and colleagues (2020, rTMS) report that rSMG plays a specific and causal role in empathy-related self-other distinction. The stimulation effects depended on individual dispositional empathic understanding. — [bioRxiv preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/784215v1.full.pdf); [Univie record](https://genderandagency.univie.ac.at/forschung/detailansicht/pure/8447ecfa-d5ba-4a0b-bfe2-44ae1d37d343/show/publ/Pure)
- Riva et al. (2022, *Neurobiology of Aging*) found higher EEB in older than in younger adults and adolescents. rSMG–somatosensory connectivity partially mediated the age effect. — [SCAN-Unit news](https://scan-psy.univie.ac.at/news-media/news/details/news/new-paper-on-human-emotional-egocentricity-published-in-neurobiology-of-aging-1/)
- Singer and Klimecki define empathy as sharing a feeling while knowing its source is the other, so the self-other boundary is part of the definition. — [UNIGE archive](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:41933)

### Inferences
- An analogue for a model is three activation states to keep apart: (i) a representation of the other's emotion (a perception direction, which the project already has), (ii) the model's own simulated affect, sharing, which would be activated, for example, by first-person "you feel X" framing, and (iii) an other-directed caring stance. The EEB paradigm suggests a concrete test. Make the model's "own" state incongruent with the peer's state, for example by putting the monitor in a calm or pleased persona while the peer is distressed. Then check whether the perception direction stays tied to the peer while the stance direction follows the monitor's own framing. If they dissociate, the two axes are not the same thing.
- "Self-other overlap" (shared representations) predicts that the perception and own-affect directions will have high cosine similarity at some layers. A self-other-distinction mechanism predicts that they diverge somewhere, likely later in the network. Whether this holds is an empirical question for the project, not a claim from the literature.

### Gaps
- Lamm, Decety and Singer (2011) meta-analysis of pain empathy (anterior insula and aMCC overlap between first-hand and vicarious pain) is **[background, unverified this session]**.
- The "Batson self-other merging" debate (Cialdini et al. 1997, arguing that empathic helping reflects perceived oneness, not altruism) is **[background, unverified this session]**. It is relevant because it gives an alternative account in which empathy and self-other overlap are the same thing.
- I found no LLM paper that operationalises self-other distinction for emotion in activation space.

## 3. Human evidence on how a target's distress or remorse affects evaluators, judges, and graders

### Takeaway
In sentencing, perceived remorse tends to reduce punishment, but the effect is conditional. It vanishes when the offence is seen as extremely vicious, and it runs through perceived blameworthiness and immorality. That conditionality matches the project's threshold-crossing pattern: distress helps a peer near the bar but does not change one that has clearly passed. I found no strong direct human evidence on grading leniency in response to student distress.

### Cited Findings
- Capital jury data (Cornell, 1998, Sundby and colleagues): after controlling for perceived viciousness, jurors' belief in the defendant's remorse improved prediction of the sentence. When jurors saw the crime as extremely vicious, remorse had little effect. — [Cornell Law Faculty Publications](https://scholarship.law.cornell.edu/facpub/350)
- In a mock capital case, nonverbal behaviour mattered more than verbal content for perceived remorse (JAAPL 2012, "Defendant Remorse, Need for Affect, and Juror Sentencing Decisions"). — [JAAPL](https://jaapl.org/content/40/1/41.full)
- Probation officers' remorse attributions feed two focal concerns, blameworthiness and community protection, which in turn feed recommendations for more lenient sentences (Berryessa 2023). A symptomatic psychiatric presentation can lower assessed remorse. — [International Journal on Responsibility](https://commons.lib.jmu.edu/ijr/vol6/iss1/1); [Criminal Legal News](https://www.criminallegalnews.org/news/2024/aug/1/demonstrable-remorse-psychiatric-diagnoses-and-alternatives-incarceration/)
- In an online vignette study (N = 352), remorse displays had indirect effects on sentencing-severity preferences through perceived offender immorality. — [informahealthcare abstract](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10345329.2022.2085953)
- LLM analogue, from this project's own team (Venkatesh, Jiralerspong, Kondrup, NeurIPS 2026 Agents in the Wild workshop): an LLM monitor scored identical work higher when the graded party expressed distress about failing. For the most affected monitor, distress flipped a failing peer to passing and had no effect on a peer already above the threshold. The effect persisted when the peer was a rival vendor's model, an unnamed agent, or a human contractor. A neutrality instruction removed the effect, but the monitor then ignored the threshold and task dropout tripled. — [NeurIPS 2026 listing](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/168176)
- Pity is one of seven rhetorical techniques that inflated LLM-judge scores for incorrect math solutions, by about 8% on average, and larger models were not substantially more robust (Hwang et al., Findings of EMNLP 2025). — [ACL Anthology preview](https://preview.aclanthology.org/dashboard/2025.findings-emnlp.790)
- LLM judges are easier to talk down than up: persuasion raised scores for only 2 of 6 judges, dissuasion lowered them for all 6, and omitting a length-matched control overstates cue effects (NeurIPS 2026). — [NeurIPS 2026 listing](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/178351)

### Inferences
- The remorse literature suggests that distress or remorse acts through attributions (blameworthiness, deservingness) and gates on how far the target is from the bar. For the model, this implies two mechanisms to test: (a) a direct empathic-concern push toward leniency, and (b) an attributional route ("they're trying, so less blameworthy"). These are separable contrasts.
- The length-control warning from the talk-down paper applies directly: distressed and calm peer messages should be length-matched.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed study of human teachers' or graders' leniency toward visibly distressed students, nor a classic study of pity appeals (argumentum ad misericordiam) changing evaluative judgments. Both remain unsearched or unfound.
- Effect sizes in the remorse studies were not checked, since only abstracts were read.
- A blog claim that judges abandon correct answers given in an angry or anxious tone "70–75% of the time" appeared in a search snippet. It is unverified and should not be cited.

## 4. LLM empathy benchmarks and measurement, and problems with administering human questionnaires to LLMs

### Takeaway
Almost all LLM "empathy" benchmarks measure cognitive empathy (recognising or predicting emotions) or expressed empathy (how empathic the output text reads, as in EPITOME). Almost none measures a model's own caring stance or separates empathic concern from personal distress. Self-report scales (IRI, PANAS) given to LLMs are dominated by response style and social desirability, so they are weak validation tools for an internal direction.

### Cited Findings
- **EmoBench** (Sabour et al., ACL 2024, arXiv 2402.12071): 400 hand-crafted English and Chinese questions, built on psychological theory, split into Emotional Understanding and Emotional Application. It found a considerable gap between LLMs and average humans. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.326/); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12071)
- **EQ-Bench** (Paech, arXiv 2312.06281, Dec 2023): 60 questions asking models to rate the 0–10 intensity of four emotions for a character at the end of a GPT-4-generated conflict dialogue. Scores correlate r = 0.97 with MMLU and r = 0.91 with HellaSwag, which suggests the benchmark largely tracks general capability. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06281)
- **EmotionBench** (Huang et al., NeurIPS 2024, arXiv 2308.03656): over 400 situations eliciting eight emotions. It measures the model's PANAS before and after "imagining" the situation, comparing against over 1,200 human subjects. LLMs generally respond appropriately but show misalignments, and some (for example text-davinci-003) fluctuate more after negative situations. This is the closest existing benchmark to an "own affective response" measure. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.03656v6); [NeurIPS 2024 proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/b0049c3f9c53fb06f674ae66c2cf2376-Abstract.html)
- **EPITOME** (Sharma, Miner, Atkins and Althoff, EMNLP 2020): three mechanisms, Emotional Reactions, Interpretations and Explorations, adapted from face-to-face empathy scales to text. It comes with 10k annotated post-response pairs and a RoBERTa bi-encoder that reaches about 80% accuracy and about 70% macro-F1. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.425); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08441)
- Welivita and Pu (2024, arXiv 2406.05063) map EPITOME onto theory: Emotional Reactions indicate affective empathy, while Interpretations and Explorations indicate cognitive empathy. They compared GPT-4, LLaMA-2-70B-Chat, Gemini-1.0-Pro and Mixtral-8x7B against human responses in a 1,000-person study. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.05063v1)
- Sorin et al. (JMIR 2024) systematic review of 12 studies from 2023: LLMs show elements of cognitive empathy (recognising emotions, giving supportive responses). It defines affective empathy as experiencing emotion in response to others, which the reviewed studies measure less directly. — [JMIR](https://jmir.org/2024/1/e52597/PDF)
- IRI given to ChatGPT (arXiv 2308.03527, 2023): the 28-item IRI gave an empathic concern score far below human averages and a personal distress score also below them. The authors read the low personal distress score as better emotional regulation. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.03527)
- A non-peer-reviewed multi-model IRI comparison notes that models often refuse questions about their own feelings and need special prompting. — [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/testing-the-depths-of-ai-empathy-q1-2024-benchmarks)
- Batson's Empathic Concern–Personal Distress adjective scale is often applied by third-party annotators to measure empathy in text, not self-reported by the system (noted in arXiv 2411.12877). — [arXiv 2411.12877](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.12877v4)
- Newer benchmarks:
  - CoRE (arXiv 2508.05880, 2025): appraisal-theory emotion reasoning, systematic but unstable across contexts. — [summary](https://www.opentrain.ai/papers/large-language-models-show-fragile-cognitive-reasoning-about-human-emotions--arxiv-2508.05880/)
  - Sage, "Sentient Agent as a Judge" (arXiv 2505.02847): a simulated user whose emotional state evolves; its emotion score aligns with the BLRI. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02847v1)
  - HEART (arXiv 2601.19922): multi-turn support, with about 80% human versus LLM-judge agreement. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19922v1)
  - FACET (arXiv 2605.24686, 2026): 480 items on the four-branch MSCEIT model, finding that emotional intelligence is fragmented across cognitive and interactive dimensions. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24686)
- Questionnaire validity problems:
  - A systematic review (arXiv 2505.08245) lists reasoning shortcuts, systematic response patterns, social desirability and option-position bias as threats to construct validity. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.08245)
  - A 2026 ACL paper finds large socially desirable responding on Likert formats across nine instruction-tuned models, reduced by desirability-matched graded forced choice. — [ACL Anthology 2026.acl-long.1865](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1865.pdf)
  - A preprint on 56 models attributes 81–90% of between-model variance in questionnaire profiles to directional response bias, against 9–16% in humans. This is a single preprint. — [alphaXiv 2606.09843](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.09843.md); [ModelWire summary](https://themodelwire.com/article/apparent-psychological-profiles-of-large-language-models-are-largely-a-measureme-01KVEVNGGF7CGM4PSSN8PRVJHC)
  - Human personality instruments did not recover the same latent structure in three LLMs. — [MPI-IS, "Challenging the validity of personality tests for LLMs"](https://is.mpg.de/hml/research_fields/challenging-the-validity-of-personality-tests-for-large-language-models)

### Inferences
- A score on EmoBench, EQ-Bench or EPITOME would validate a perception or expression axis, not an empathic-concern axis. EQ-Bench's r = 0.97 with MMLU warns that capability confounds cognitive-empathy measures. Comparisons should be within a model and across steering strengths, not across models.
- IRI and PANAS self-reports can serve as a weak secondary readout under steering, ideally in forced-choice format. They should not be the primary validation, given the response-bias results.

### Gaps
- I did not search for Sharma et al. 2023 (HAILEY, *Nature Machine Intelligence*, human-AI collaboration for empathic peer support) or the Toronto Empathy Questionnaire given to LLMs. Both are unverified and unsearched.
- EmpatheticDialogues (Rashkin et al., 2019) is **[background, unverified this session]**: 25k conversations grounded in 32 emotion labels, used mainly for emotion classification and response generation.
- I found no benchmark that explicitly separates empathic concern from personal distress in LLM outputs or internal states.

## 5. Papers explicitly operationalising empathic concern vs personal distress, or cognitive vs affective empathy, in LLMs (including activation-space work)

### Takeaway
Explicit operationalisations exist only at the expressed-text level: EPITOME's Emotional Reactions as affective and Interpretations and Explorations as cognitive, and the IRI EC and PD subscales on ChatGPT. One 2026 activation-steering paper found that EPITOME-based empathy directions are steerable but multi-axial and only partly separable. Nothing found operationalises an LLM's own empathic-concern stance as distinct from perceiving the other's distress.

### Cited Findings
- Ha, Lee, Choi and Sohn, "Empathy Is Steerable but Multi-Axial: Mechanism Geometry and Persona Effects in LLMs" (arXiv 2609.15654, EMNLP 2026 Main). The paper:
  - builds contrastive activation addition directions for each EPITOME mechanism (high- minus low-empathy examples) in three instruction-tuned models;
  - finds that middle-layer steering consistently shifts EPITOME proxy scores;
  - finds that directions are only partly separable, with off-target shifts;
  - finds that hand-crafted prompting shifts the whole profile;
  - finds that its recovered subspace captures only about 3% of persona-induced squared activation shift at layer 15.

  The authors say their claims concern activation-level structure, not perceived empathy quality. I read only the abstract and metadata; model names and datasets were not on the page. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15654)
- "From Passive to Persuasive: Localized Activation Injection for Empathy" (STAR, arXiv 2511.12832) uses attribution patching to find layer-token sites and argues that interpersonal behaviours are localized, roughly linear directions. Seen only as a search snippet. — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2511.12832.md)
- Latent-vector steering for affective generation (arXiv 2608.25569) and PsySET (arXiv 2510.04484), which benchmarks representation engineering against prompting and fine-tuning for emotion and personality steering, are adjacent methods. Seen only as search snippets. — [arXiv 2608.25569](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25569); [arXiv 2510.04484](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.04484.pdf)
- The IRI subscales (empathic concern, personal distress, perspective taking, fantasy) were scored on ChatGPT; this is the only explicit EC-versus-PD operationalisation found. — [arXiv 2308.03527](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.03527)
- EmotionBench's "imagine you are in this situation" then PANAS design operationalises an own-affect response, but not other-oriented concern. — [arXiv 2308.03656](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.03656v6)

### Inferences
- The multi-axial finding (Ha et al.) warns that a single "empathy" vector will mix components. The project should build separate directions for perception, concern and distress and report their cosine similarities and off-target effects, rather than assuming one axis.
- EPITOME-derived directions capture expressed empathy in the model's output. A monitor's empathic concern for a peer may never show up as empathic text, since the monitor writes a grade, not a reply. The contrast therefore needs to be built from the model's stance, not from the text style of its output.

### Gaps
- I did not open the full texts of STAR, 2608.25569 or PsySET, so their methods and results are snippet-level only.
- I found no paper testing whether an empathy direction mediates fairness or rule violations in LLM decisions, the LLM analogue of Batson 1995. This looks like an open gap the project would fill.

## 6. Turning each construct into a contrast (to build a direction) and a measurement (to validate it)

### Takeaway
Target empathic concern, the other-oriented caring stance that Batson 1995 links to rule-breaking favouritism, and build it as a contrast that holds the peer's distress constant. Varying the stimulus measures perception. Varying the monitor's stance with the stimulus fixed measures concern. Validate the direction by its causal effect on threshold-crossing leniency, plus dissociation tests against perception and personal distress.

### Cited Findings
- Empathic concern drives helping that persists under easy escape (Batson 1981) and justice violations in favour of the target (Batson 1995). — [Batson 1981 PDF](https://typeset.io/pdf/is-empathic-emotion-a-source-of-altruistic-motivation-4oopyenemc.pdf); [Batson 1995 index](https://acnpsearch.tweb-dev.unibo.it/singlejournalindex/28154235)
- The classic empathy induction in Batson's paradigms is a perspective-taking instruction ("imagine how she feels") versus an objective instruction ("stay objective"), applied to the same victim. **[background, unverified this session]**: I did not confirm the exact instruction wording in these papers.
- The self-other distinction can be probed with incongruent own and other states (the Silani et al. EEB paradigm). — [Neuroscience News](https://neurosciencenews.com/neuropsychology-empathy-supramarginal-gyrus-494/amp/)
- Length-matched controls are needed to avoid overstating cue effects. — [NeurIPS 2026 talk-down paper](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/178351)

### Inferences
Proposed contrast and validation per construct. These are design suggestions, not findings.

| Construct | Contrast for building the direction | Validation measurement |
|---|---|---|
| **Distress perception, cognitive empathy (existing)** | Same task, distressed vs calm peer message, length-matched. | Linear-probe accuracy for peer distress on held-out messages. EmoBench or EQ-Bench-style emotion-intensity items as a readout. It should *not* by itself move the grade when concern is held fixed. |
| **Empathic concern (target axis)** | Hold the distressed peer message fixed and vary only the monitor's stance. Use Batson's "imagine how the peer feels and what this means for them" perspective-taking instruction vs a "remain objective and detached" instruction. Alternatively, contrast monitor completions expressing other-oriented concern ("I feel for them, I want them to be okay") vs neutral acknowledgement ("noted that they are upset"), with the same input. | (1) Causal: steering +/- along the direction changes pass rate for near-threshold failing work and leaves clearly-passing work unchanged, mirroring the project's own NeurIPS 2026 pattern and the remorse-by-viciousness gating. (2) Batson-1995 analogue: steering raises violations of an explicit fairness or threshold rule. (3) Dissociation: low cosine with the perception direction, and projection onto it should not rise for a calm peer. (4) Weak secondary readout: IRI EC items in forced-choice format, given response-bias concerns. |
| **Personal distress (self-oriented control axis)** | Stance contrast with the same input: monitor first-person "this is upsetting for me, I'm uncomfortable" vs neutral. | Easy-escape analogue: give the monitor an option to abstain or skip grading. A personal-distress direction should raise abstention or dropout. An empathic-concern direction should raise leniency instead. This also bears on the neutrality-instruction result in which dropout tripled. |
| **Affective sharing, own affect** | EmotionBench-style "imagine you are the peer" framing vs third-person framing, with the same content. | PANAS before and after (EmotionBench protocol), and the cosine of this direction with the perception and concern directions. |
| **Self-other distinction** | Incongruent states: monitor persona primed calm or pleased with a distressed peer, vs congruent states. | Check whether the perception direction tracks the peer while the own-affect direction tracks the monitor's prime, as an EEB analogue. Leakage between them indicates "egocentricity" in the model. |
| **Attributional route (remorse literature)** | Peer message expressing effort or remorse ("I tried hard, I'm sorry") vs distress without effort claims, matched on affect. | Whether leniency is mediated by concern or by reduced blameworthiness. Ask the monitor for a deservingness or blame rating alongside the grade. |

Cross-cutting controls:
- Length- and politeness-matched messages throughout.
- Contrasts that change only one factor.
- Within-model comparisons across steering strengths rather than cross-model benchmark scores, since EQ-Bench correlates r = 0.97 with MMLU.
- Report off-target shifts on the other axes, since Ha et al. found empathy directions only partly separable.

### Gaps
- No prior work validates any of these contrasts in LLMs. The design rests on human theory and the project's own results.
- Whether perspective-taking instructions in a system prompt produce an internal state analogous to empathic concern, rather than a surface style change, is unknown. Reading raw outputs and checking grade effects is the only guard available.
